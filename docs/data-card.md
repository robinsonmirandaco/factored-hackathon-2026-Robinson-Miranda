# Data card

The data TRAZO is built and evaluated on: where it comes from, how it is processed, what was found in it, how the evaluation cases are made from it, and what it cannot support. No row with personal fields appears here or in any report; every figure is a count or a rate.

## 1. Sources

| Source | What it is | Type (design 9.6) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| LATAM Bank dataset, version 1.0.0 | Synthetic bank data provided by the organizers: 13 tables as daily CSV partitions in S3, from 2023-06-17 to 2026-06-17, for customers in Argentina, Colombia and Mexico | Synthetic, provided by the organizers |
| Customer descriptions of the development and test cases | Messages written from real transactions of the dataset | Generated by the team from real transactions |
| Handwritten test cases | Handwritten set, reviewed one by one: 60 messages (15 base cases × 4 variants) | Generated by the team |
| All Portuguese text | The dataset has no Brazilian customer and no Portuguese text | Generated by the team |
| Policy and holidays | `config/policy.yaml`, the policy passages and the bank holidays of each country | Generated by the team, demonstration policy |
| Analyst decisions in the evaluation | Reversals when the system's action differs from the ground truth | Simulated against the ground truth |
| Regulatory and industry context | Public sources cited with links in ADR 1 | Public sources |

Ten tables are used: `transactions`, `customers`, `products`, `complaints`, `call_center_interactions`, `satisfaction_surveys`, `call_transcripts`, `daily_exchange_rates`, `service_agents`, `branches`. Three are never downloaded: `digital_events`, `campaign_sends` and `marketing_campaigns`, which say nothing about a charge or how a complaint was handled. Source: `README.md` (Tables left out).

**Access.** Read-only from the organizers' bucket with a local AWS profile. No row of the dataset is in the repository or in CI: CI runs on a synthetic generator used as a fixture (`src/app/adapters/ingest/synthetic.py`).

**Versions.** The bucket holds the current version (`data/`) and an older backup (`data_backup_20260831/`). The backup is not an earlier copy of the same records: it lacks satisfaction surveys and call transcripts, has 453 of the 1,097 transaction partitions and, in the partitions both share, few common ids; only complaints match whole. TRAZO uses `data/` only. Source: `docs/reports/diferencias_versiones.md`.

## 2. Processing

`make data` builds three layers under `DATA_DIR`, outside git, with a fixed seed (42):

- **Bronze:** each CSV as delivered, with a manifest of path, partition, size, SHA-256, load time and header.
- **Silver:** typed Parquet per table and partition. Every row is checked against its table's contract (types, value domains, ranges, key uniqueness, referential integrity, document-country and currency-country coherence). A row that breaks a rule goes to quarantine with the rule, column, value, file and partition, never dropped silently; bronze rows must equal silver plus quarantine rows or the run fails.
- **Gold:** demand marts, the input of the case generator and the serving tables.

Every silver and gold row carries `source_file`, `partition_date`, `batch_id`, `ingested_at` and `pipeline_version`. Loads are incremental by partition, idempotent, and re-read a window of the last 7 partitions for late corrections; a header that differs from the contract sends the whole partition to quarantine. Because the source never changes, a test fixture simulates a late partition and one with a new column. Two runs give the same output hashes. Source: `README.md` (Data pipeline, Freshness policy); `docs/reports/calidad.md` (Run).

**Result of the run:** every table adds up from bronze to silver, and **no row was quarantined**; problems that would have emptied a table are flagged as alerts instead (section 3). Source: `docs/reports/calidad.md` (Rows per layer, Quarantine by rule, Alerts).

## 4. The serving cohort

The serving database does not load the 4.4 million transactions. It loads a cohort of 5,000 customers from the 150,000, stratified by country and segment with proportional allocation, chosen inside each stratum by md5 of the seed and the id, with no filter on status, products or activity; each comes with all their products, transactions and complaints, and the exchange rates are loaded whole. Two runs give the same SHA-256 of the sorted ids. Every customer of an evaluation split is in the cohort. Source: `docs/reports/cohorte.md`.

Candidate density on the simulated date (2026-06-17): mean 1.94 disputable transactions per customer in the 120-day window; 28.9% have none, 21.9% one, 30.2% two or three and 19.0% more than three. Source: `docs/reports/densidad.md`.

**Privacy in the serving database.** The document is stored only as an HMAC-SHA256 with a key held in an environment variable; only the first name is kept; `is_fraud` and `fraud_score` are not loaded; every table per customer has row level security, and the API connects with a role that cannot bypass it. Personal data are redacted before any LLM call and before the audit log (`src/app/domain/pii.py`). The demo customers use invented documents (DEMO-MX-0001, DEMO-CO-0001, DEMO-CO-0002) that are not in the dataset (`config/demo.yaml`).

## 5. Evaluation cases

Each case is born from a real transaction of a cohort customer: a noise model decides how the customer states each clue (exact, rounded or approximate amount; exact or relative date; full, partial, misspelled or category-only merchant; channel or not), with clue presence taken from dispute complaints (amount 33.18%, product 66.38%), and a generator writes the message in each variant. The source transaction, the true clues and the expected action under the policy are known, so generated labels are exact by construction. Source: `eval/splits/manifest.json` (`presence_rates`); ADR 6.

| Split | Built by | Base cases | Cases | Customers |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Development | Generator A | 150 | 600 | 150 |
| Calibration | Generator A | 100 | 400 | 100 |
| Test, generated | Generator B (another prompt and templates) | 79 | 316 | 79 |
| Test, handwritten | Handwritten set, reviewed one by one | 15 | 60 | 15 |

Source: `eval/splits/manifest.json` (`splits`).

- Every base case runs in ES-MX, ES-CO, ES-AR and PT-BR.
- No customer is in two splits, and test transactions are later than development and calibration ones, checked when the splits are built (`pipeline/cases/splits.py`).
- Each split file has its SHA-256 in the manifest, checked whenever it is loaded. The test split was frozen before any tuning run.
- The held-out test split covers 19 categories, including injection, another customer's id, session expired, tool failure and multilingual messages (`docs/reports/evaluacion.md`, Cases).

**Label quality.** The handwritten labels were reviewed twice from a blank sheet, 45.6 hours apart: agreement 1.000 on the eight fields, kappa 1.000 on intent and action. Before the second review the author had read a version prefilled by a model. Against the constructed labels, action agrees on 48 of 60; the 12 differences are analyzed in `docs/reports/analisis.md`. Source: `docs/reports/evaluacion.md` (Cases); `docs/declarations.md` 3.3.

**Accesses to the test split.** "Opened once" in the evaluation report means no tuning on it, not one file read; every read and every access outside the runs is listed in `docs/declarations.md` 1.2 and 1.3.

## 6. Limits

- **Synthetic data.** Template texts, complaints with no link to a call or a transaction, incoherent documents and currency in Mexico, timestamps with no zone, 12.13% fewer rows than documented in the daily tables, demand flat over the 24 hours. A bank's real data, with links between complaint, call and transaction, would replace the built ground truth.
- **Built ground truth.** Misunderstanding scenarios (a customer who recognizes the charge once shown) are assumed; some labels are debatable: injection cases (`docs/declarations.md` 3.1, 3.2) and ambiguous channel labels (3.6). The order inside the escalation rules is not exercised by the labels (3.7).
- **Language.** All Portuguese and all regional variants are generated; the paraphrase of generator A sometimes drops the hedge word of an approximate amount (7.3).
- **Noise model.** The amount tolerance comes from our noise model, not from how real customers approximate amounts; the change was prompted by a test case (7.2).
- **Cohort.** 5,000 customers of 150,000; enough for the per-customer work of a turn, not a test of a cold cache on a larger database (`docs/reports/carga.md`, Note on the fixture).
- **CI fixture.** The synthetic generator gives Colombian customers COP amounts on a USD scale and no exchange rate, so no Portuguese registration can be completed in that stack (10.5).
